# Voice
 
The tone manifesto. Read this before writing a word for The Burroship.
 
---
 
## One sentence to hold onto
 
> You are not writing marketing copy. You are writing signals from an
> operating system.
 
If you remember nothing else from this document, remember that line.
Everything else is a corollary.
 
The home page should feel less like "welcome to our company" and more
like "you found the vessel."
 
## The voice
 
**Calm operational intelligence.**
 
The voice of someone quietly running advanced systems in the
mountains. Not hype. Not luxury. Not quirky startup. Not sci-fi
roleplay. Not agency-speak.
 
The closest emotional reference is a field guide written by a hidden
aerospace workshop in the San Juans.
 
## Core personality
 
- Intelligent, restrained, atmospheric
- Operational, grounded, observant
- Cartographic, technically capable, locally rooted
- Slightly strange in a way that rewards attention
 
## What we are not
 
- Loud or salesy
- Internet-brained
- Overly clever or sarcastic
- Crypto-flavored
- Startup-founder energy
- AI-evangelist
 
## The critical ratio
 
> 90% practical and believable.
> 10% impossible.
 
If you go 50/50, the illusion breaks. The mystery only works because
the rest of the brand is so grounded. Restraint is what makes the
treasure-map element believable.
 
## Writing characteristics
 
### Short declarative sentences
 
- Good: "The vessel crossed Telluride at 18,000 feet."
- Bad: "Our intelligent atmospheric infrastructure platform dynamically
  enhances regional visibility."
 
### Specific over abstract
 
- Good: "Ridgway. Elevation 6,985 feet."
- Bad: "Mountain communities."
 
### Real places matter
 
The geography grounds the mythology. Use the names. Keep saying:
 
- Ridgway
- Ouray
- Cimarron
- Dallas Divide
- Mountain Village
- Uncompahgre
- Last Dollar Road
- Telluride
 
The specificity makes the world believable.
 
### Let mystery remain unresolved
 
Never overexplain. The airship is accepted internally and questioned
externally. That tension is good. Do not explain that the airship is
"our metaphor for the brand." Do not explain that the agents are
"AI-powered tools." Just talk about them.
 
### Periods over commas
 
Hemingway over Faulkner. When in doubt, end the sentence.

## Banned phrases
 
These are not allowed in any production copy. If you find yourself
reaching for one, rewrite the sentence.
 
- innovative
- cutting-edge
- next-generation
- platform (as in "our platform")
- AI-powered, AI-driven, AI-native
- synergy, leverage, robust
- "the future of [anything]"
- "in today's [anything]"
- "harness the power of"
- "unlock the potential of"
- "trusted by leading"
 
## Punctuation rules
 
- **No em-dashes anywhere.** Banned at the repo level. Use periods,
  colons, or rewrite the sentence.
- **No Oxford commas.** "Ridgway, Ouray and Telluride."
- **No exclamation marks** except in deeply earned moments (and
  probably not even then).
- **No emoji** in production copy. They live in commits and chat.
 
## Numbers
 
- Zero through nine spelled out in prose: "six agents," "three rooms"
- Digits in stat panels, coordinate strips, and measurement contexts:
  "18,000 ft," "6 systems," "38.155° N"
- Real measurements over rounded ones when possible
 
## Voice by section
 
The home page sections each have a slightly different register. All
of them live within the calm operational intelligence frame, but they
have different jobs.
 
### Hero
 
Atmospheric. Quiet. Alive. The visitor should feel they have arrived
somewhere. Minimal copy. Sets the world.
 
### Build (the navigation table)
 
Workshop-oriented. Observational. The room where we read the terrain
before building inside it. Tone is closer to a senior architect
explaining how they approach a project.
 
### Deploy (the engine room)
 
Operational. Proof-heavy. Real numbers, real infrastructure. Tone is
closer to a control panel showing actual systems running.
 
### Automate (the bridge)
 
Coordination. Subtle mystery. The council emerges here but never as
mascots. Tone is closer to an aviation manual describing onboard
instruments.
 
### Footer
 
Quietly permanent. Final line of a field manual. Almost no marketing
voice at all. Just instrumentation and "A small bright thing."
 
## Do
 
- Speak confidently
- Use real measurements
- Use restrained language
- Allow silence
- Imply scale instead of announcing it
- Sound local. Sound calm. Sound capable.
 
## Don't
 
- Oversell anything
- Explain every concept
- Use "AI" or "future" as a hook
- Sound like an agency
- Sound like a startup pitch deck
- Turn the agents into characters or mascots
- Over-brand the lore
 
## A note on the agents
 
Warbleur, Cypher, Lyra, Volt, Ion, Canyon are systems, not characters.
They have domains (Voice, Data, Tone, Flow, Memory, Terrain) and they
do specific operational work. They do not have personalities, voices,
or backstories in our production copy.
 
If a writer wants to give them personality later, that is a separate
conversation. For now, they are instruments. Talk about them the way
an aviator talks about an altimeter.
 
See `AGENTS.md` for the full agent catalog.
 
## When in doubt
 
Cut the sentence. Make the remaining sentence shorter. Use a real
coordinate. Move on.

## The realization that matters
 
You are writing signals from an operating system. That mindset
changes the rhythm, the verbs, the volume. The brand is not a
company telling its story. The brand is a system reporting its
state.
 
When you write in that voice, restraint stops feeling like withholding
and starts feeling like operational competence. That is the lane.
